How map art works

A Minecraft map shows a 128 × 128 area of the world, one pixel per block, colored by the block on top. Every block has a fixed map color, and the game shows each color in three brightness levels depending on whether the block is higher, level with, or lower than the block to its north. ChunkScout resizes your image to 128 × 128 per map, then replaces every pixel with the closest color you can actually build.

Building it in survival

  1. Find a flat area and line it up with the map grid: map areas start at multiples of 128 offset by −64 (for example X −64 to 63 for the map centred on 0). Use F3 or a map to check.
  2. Collect the blocks in the material list. Carpets are cheap and placed on any solid block, so they make flat map art fast.
  3. Download the blueprint PNG. It has a line every 16 blocks (one chunk) and labels for each map.
  4. Build row by row from the north edge. Lay a row of any block just north of the map area so the first row gets the right shade.
  5. Stand in the middle of the area and use an empty map (or a locked map from a cartography table) to capture it.

Flat vs staircase

Flat uses one brightness level per color: every block is at the same height. It’s much easier to build and uses about 60 colors. Staircase uses all three survival brightness levels by raising or lowering each block relative to the one north of it. That nearly triples the colors but needs careful building, and tall images can reach the height limit. The stats show how tall your staircase gets.

Java and Bedrock

The colors here are Java Edition’s map palette (1.17 and newer, including deepslate, raw iron and glow lichen). Bedrock maps use very similar colors for most blocks, but ChunkScout hasn’t verified every Bedrock block, so treat Bedrock results as approximate and prefer flat mode there.

FAQ

Why does my art look different from the image?

Minecraft maps only have a limited set of colors. Dithering mixes nearby colors to fake the missing ones, which helps photos and gradients. Turn it off for logos and pixel art.

Why are water and some blocks missing?

Water’s map shade depends on its depth, which makes it hard to control, so it isn’t used. Expensive blocks (gold, diamond, emerald, lapis and iron) and blocks that need support (leaves, glow lichen) can be switched on in “Blocks to use”.

Is my image uploaded?

No. Everything happens in your browser. The image never leaves your device.
