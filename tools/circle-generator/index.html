How to build a circle in Minecraft

  1. Pick the diameter you want and a style. Thin outlines are the usual choice for walls and towers.
  2. Mark the centre in-game. Odd diameters have one centre block. Even diameters have a 2×2 centre.
  3. Build the four straight edges first (the longest runs at the top, bottom, left and right), then fill in the diagonals using the row guide.
  4. Use Zoom view, or download the blueprint and keep it open on a second screen or your phone.

Odd or even diameter?

Odd diameters (like 21) are symmetrical around a single block, so they’re easier to centre on a doorway, a beacon or a pillar. Even diameters (like 20) look slightly rounder at small sizes but need a 2×2 centre.

Thin, thick and filled

Thin outlines connect only through corners in the diagonal parts, which uses the fewest blocks. Thick outlines connect along edges everywhere, so mobs and water can’t slip through and walls look solid from inside. Filled gives you a floor or a pond shape.

Spheres, domes and towers

For a tower, repeat the same thin circle on every layer. For a dome, stack circles that shrink as you go up: each layer’s diameter follows the curve of a semicircle with the dome’s width. Draw that semicircle here (set width to your dome width and use a filled style), then read off each row’s length as the diameter for that layer.
