How to build a circle in Minecraft

  1. Pick the diameter you want and a style. Thin outlines are the usual choice for walls and towers.
  2. Mark the centre in-game. Odd diameters have one centre block. Even diameters have a 2×2 centre.
  3. Build the four straight edges first (the longest runs at the top, bottom, left and right), then fill in the diagonals using the row guide.
  4. Zoom in to read the block numbers, or download the PNG and keep it open on a second screen or your phone. Copy link saves the exact circle so you can reopen or share it.

Diameter or radius?

The diameter is how many blocks wide the circle is. The radius is half of that, so a radius of 10.5 gives a 21-block circle and a radius of 10 gives a 20-block circle. Circles from 3 to 500 blocks wide are supported.

Odd or even diameter?

Odd diameters (like 21) are symmetrical around a single block, so they’re easier to centre on a doorway, a beacon or a pillar. Even diameters (like 20) look slightly rounder at small sizes but need a 2×2 centre.

Thin, thick and filled

Thin outlines connect only through corners in the diagonal parts, which uses the fewest blocks. Thick outlines connect along full edges everywhere (no corner-only joins), so walls look solid from inside and water can’t flow out diagonally. Filled gives you a floor or a pond shape.

How the circles are calculated

Each block is treated as a 1×1 square. A block is inside the shape when its centre point lies inside the ellipse:

((x + 0.5 − W/2) ÷ (W/2))² + ((y + 0.5 − H/2) ÷ (H/2))² ≤ 1
  • Filled uses every inside block.
  • Thick keeps inside blocks that touch an outside block on any side or corner, so every block joins the next along a full edge.
  • Thin starts from the blocks that touch the outside on one of their four sides and removes any that are already covered by a neighbour toward the centre on both axes, leaving a single-block line that connects at the corners.

Because the test is applied to every block in the same way, the result is always symmetrical in all four quarters.

Spheres, domes and towers

For a tower, repeat the same thin circle on every layer. For a dome, stack circles that shrink as you go up: each layer’s diameter follows the curve of a semicircle with the dome’s width. Draw that semicircle here (set width to your dome width and use a filled style), then read off each row’s length as the diameter for that layer.
